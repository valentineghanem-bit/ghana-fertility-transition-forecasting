# Evidence — LITERATURE REVIEW (fertility transition & determinants)

- **SSA stall — contested.** Stalls first identified in **Ghana** & Kenya (early 2000s); on
  rigorous reanalysis stalls are unambiguous only in Namibia/Zimbabwe and Ghana is NOT a confirmed
  case — "not widespread but not exceptional". — Schoumaker 2019, *Stud Fam Plann*
  [Consensus: ACTIVE DEBATE; CONTRASTING re: Ghana].
- **Stalls overstated (CONTRASTING).** Many reported stalls are data-quality artefacts (age
  displacement, omission); compelling stall only in Kenya. — Machiyama 2010 [CONTRASTING].
- **Determinants.** Female education, contraceptive use, child-mortality decline, fertility
  preferences. — Shapiro & Tenikue 2013. Multilevel social determinants of young-women FP use in
  Ghana. — Challa et al. 2017, *Women Health*, DOI 10.1080/03630242.2017.1306607.
- **Urbanisation.** Urban expansion co-occurs with rising mCPR and fertility decline across 6
  African countries. — Lei et al. 2026, *Afr J Reprod Health*, DOI 10.29063/ajrh2026/v30i8.6.

- **Education as dominant determinant.** Women's education has a stronger effect on fertility
  desires than wealth or residence across 34 SSA countries. — Kebede et al. 2021, *Popul Stud*.
  Education + FP programs drive both wanted and unwanted fertility decline. — Bongaarts 2020, *Genus*.
- **Stall ↔ education (mechanism).** Africa's fertility stalls partly result from disruptions in
  female-education cohorts after political/economic turmoil (~up to 0.5 child/woman by 2010).
  — Kebede et al. 2019, *PNAS* — links the stall debate to a measurable cause.
- **Adolescent SRH determinants.** Parent–adolescent communication, fear framing, and absent
  fathers shape SSA adolescent SRH. — Usonwu et al. 2021, *Reprod Health*, DOI 10.1186/s12978-021-01246-0.
- **Regional comparator.** Benin unmet need 38% with strong regional variation. — Yeboah et al. 2024,
  *Int Health*, DOI 10.1093/inthealth/ihad049 [contextual].
